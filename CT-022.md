## 1. Governing Equations & Microgravity Boundary Limits

In $1\text{-G}$ terrestrial systems, buoyancy-driven natural convection dominates multiphase separation. In microgravity environments ($g \approx 10^{-6}\text{ m/s}^2$), the Bond number ($Bo$), representing the ratio of gravitational forces to surface tension forces, approaches zero:

$$Bo = \frac{\Delta \rho \cdot g \cdot L^2}{\gamma} \ll 1$$

Where:
* $\Delta \rho$ = density difference between liquid and vapor phases
* $g$ = gravitational acceleration
* $L$ = characteristic channel length
* $\gamma$ = surface tension coefficient

### 1.1 Capillary Flow Stabilization Limit
When $Bo < 1$, surface tension $\gamma$ dominates fluid topology. Condensate removal must rely entirely on capillary channel geometry rather than gravity. Localized fluid momentum is governed by the Weber number ($We$):

$$We = \frac{\rho \cdot V^2 \cdot L}{\gamma}$$

When transient heat loads cause localized velocity spikes, $We$ exceeds the capillary hold threshold ($We > We_{\text{crit}}$), destabilizing the liquid film interface and causing liquid droplet entrainment directly into the vapor core.
